Particle Settling Simulator

Transport Phenomena in Materials Processing · MS2280 · Project 5

One rigid particle is released from rest in a stationary Newtonian fluid inside one vertical column. Weight, buoyancy and drag enter Newton's second law, ρpVp dv/dt = (ρp − ρf)gVp − FD(v), which is integrated with fourth-order Runge–Kutta. The terminal velocity comes from the selected drag law, and the wall, concentration and shape corrections can be switched on independently.

    Exports the time history of the current simulation: Time (s), Particle diameter (m), Particle density (kg/m^3), Fluid density (kg/m^3), Velocity (m/s), Position (m), Reynolds number, Drag coefficient, Drag force (N) and Net force (N). Only the acceleration phase is exported: one row per RK4 step from t = 0 to t99, when 99 % of the velocity change v₀ → vt has occurred. This is the same point at which the app reports "terminal velocity reached", and it equals 4.6τ for Stokes drag. If the particle reaches the end of the column first, the window ends there. The uniform motion at vt is not exported. The file is regenerated from the current parameters and models at the moment you export. Sign convention (notes p.2): downward is positive. Drag force is FD(v) in ρpVp dv/dt = (ρp − ρf)gVp − FD(v), so Net force = (ρp − ρf)gVp − FD = m dv/dt. With wall or concentration corrections on, FD is the effective drag used in the simulation. CD = FD/(½ρfv²Ap), left blank at v = 0.

    Physical & numerical checks

    —

      Model, equations, assumptions & sources

      Governing equations (handwritten notes)

      • Vp = πd³/6, Ap = πd²/4 (p.1)
      • W = ρpgVp, B = ρfgVp, FD = ½CDρfv²Ap (p.2)
      • ρpVp dv/dt = (ρp − ρf)gVp − FD(v), dx/dt = v, v(0) = x(0) = 0 (p.2, p.6)
      • Stokes: FD = 3πµdv, vt = Δρgd²/(18µ) (p.3)
      • Newton: CD = 0.44, vt = √[4Δρgd/(3CDρf)] (p.3)
      • Schiller–Naumann: CD = 24/Re (1 + 0.15Re0.687), Re = ρfvd/µ, solved iteratively with tolerance 10⁻⁶ (p.3–4)
      • Ar = ρfΔρgd³/µ², ReStokes = Ar/18 (p.4)
      • Francis: fw = [(1 − d/D)/(1 − 0.475d/D)]⁴; Richardson–Zaki: vh = vt(1 − φ)ⁿ (p.5)
      • Haider–Levenspiel CD and the A, B, C, K coefficients (p.5–6)
      • Stokes analytical solution: τ = ρpd²/(18µ), v = vt(1 − e−t/τ), x = vt[t − τ(1 − e−t/τ)] (p.6)
      • Energy: Pg = Δρ g Vp v = Pd = FDv at terminal velocity (p.7)

      Corrections to the notes

      • H-L coefficients C and K: the last terms use ψ³ (−10.2599ψ³, +15.8855ψ³) as in Haider & Levenspiel (1989). The notes write ψ², which gives non-physical drag for ψ < 1.
      • p.2: "W = ρpVp" is missing g (the boxed result is correct).
      • The notes' coefficient Dn is called K here, so it isn't confused with the column diameter D.
      • Drag is applied as a signed force, FD ∝ |v|v, so it opposes the motion of a rising particle as well.

      Not in the notes (stated choices)

      • Quasi-steady corrections: in the ODE, Feff(v) = FD(v/f) with f = fw·fRZ. The motion then tends exactly to f·vt. For Stokes this is FD/f, giving τeff = f·τ.
      • Combined corrections are multiplied together. This is an engineering approximation; the effects may interact.
      • t95 = τ ln 20 ≈ 3.00τ and t99 = τ ln 100 ≈ 4.61τ (from the notes' v(t)). For other drag laws these times are taken from the RK4 solution.
      • Haider–Levenspiel: d is the volume-equivalent diameter, used in both Re and Ap.
      • Initial velocity v₀ (the notes use v₀ = 0): this is the entry speed in the direction of motion, v₀ ≥ 0. The equation of motion is unchanged; only v(0) = v₀ changes. With v₀ > vt, drag exceeds W − B and the particle decelerates. The Stokes solution becomes v = vt + (v₀ − vt)e−t/τ and x = vtt + (v₀ − vt)τ(1 − e−t/τ). When v₀ ≠ 0, t95 and t99 are the times for 95 % and 99 % of the change v₀ → vt. For Stokes these are still τ ln 20 and τ ln 100.

      Assumptions

      • Rigid particle released from rest in a quiescent, incompressible, isothermal Newtonian fluid.
      • Added-mass and Basset history forces are neglected, consistent with τ = ρpd²/(18µ). Including added mass would give τ = (ρp + ρf/2)d²/(18µ).
      • No Brownian motion and no particle rotation or lateral drift. Without the wall toggle, the column wall is ignored.
      • RK4 with an adaptive step Δt = min(T, √(2H/a₀), m/(dFD/dv), H/|v|)/400, where T = vt/(|Δρ|g/ρp) is the characteristic time (equal to τ for Stokes). The step is constant from rest and for Stokes drag; it is finer during a fast deceleration from v₀ ≫ vt. Once the acceleration is negligible, the motion is continued as exactly uniform.

      Validity limits used for warnings

      • Stokes Re ≤ 0.1 (caution up to 1); Schiller–Naumann Re ≤ 800; Newton 800 ≤ Re ≤ 2×10⁵ (notes; drag crisis from Clift et al.)
      • Haider–Levenspiel Re < 25 000 and ψ ≥ 0.67 (notes p.6)
      • Francis Re ≲ 0.2, d/D ≤ 0.97; caution when d/D > 0.1 (Clift et al. 1978)
      • Richardson–Zaki φ ≤ 0.5; n = 4.65 only for Re < 0.2 (Richardson & Zaki 1954)
